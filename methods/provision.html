Service Provision¶

Overview¶

Service provision analysis allocates the demand of buildings (for example, the number of school-age children) to services with limited capacity (school places), given a matrix of travel costs between them and a normative threshold (minutes or metres). It shows which buildings are served within the threshold, which are served only by services farther away, and which services are overloaded or underused.

objectnat.get_service_provision() computes the allocation and returns a objectnat.ProvisionResult. Helper functions join the metrics back to the buildings and services and build link geometries; objectnat.recalculate_links() and objectnat.clip_provision() adjust a computed result.

Inputs¶

  • buildings: GeoDataFrame with a numeric demand column (buildings_demand_column, default "demand");

  • services: GeoDataFrame with a numeric capacity column (services_capacity_column, default "capacity");

  • distance_matrix: costs with rows buildings.index and columns services.index, in the units of threshold. It may be a dense DataFrame (inf or NaN for unreachable pairs) or a pandas sparse DataFrame (SparseDtype with NaN or inf as fill value) that stores only reachable pairs. Sparse matrices are read entry by entry without densifying, so large areas fit in memory. IduEdu’s od_matrix returns such a sparse matrix (see Building-to-service travel-time matrix).

Method¶

The allocation is a gravity-based probabilistic model; it runs in ObjectNat’s Rust extension over the entries of the (sparse) matrix. Only building–service pairs with a finite cost d of at most 3 * threshold can receive demand. Each such pair has a gravity weight 1 / (d + 1)²: the closer the building, the larger the weight, and a building twice as close gets on average about four times as much. The 1 is added so that a building at zero cost, in the same place as the service, gets a finite weight. The allocation runs in rounds:

  1. Search radius. The first round considers the pairs with a cost of up to about half the threshold, (threshold + 1) / 2; every round widens this radius 1.5 times, so nearby buildings are served first.

  2. Offers. Every service with free places takes the buildings with unmet demand inside the radius, keeps the ones with the largest weights (first the top tenth of them; the share grows as buildings and services drop out) and distributes its free places among them at random, in proportion to their weights.

  3. Acceptance. A building that got more offers than it needs accepts its unmet demand, choosing among the offers at random in proportion to their size; the rest of the places stay free.

  4. Next round. Buildings with no unmet demand and services with no free places drop out, and the rounds go on until nothing more can be allocated.

The random streams are seeded per object and iteration from seed, so a fixed seed gives the same result regardless of the number of CPU cores. Different seeds give alternative allocations with the same model.

Outputs¶

objectnat.ProvisionResult contains

  • flow: sparse building × service matrix of allocated demand;

  • demand_rows: per building demand, demand_left, supplied_demands_within and supplied_demands_without (allocated from services within / beyond threshold), min_dist (cost to the nearest service), avg_dist (flow-weighted mean cost) and provision_value = supplied_demands_within / demand;

  • capacity_rows: per service capacity, capacity_left, carried_capacity_within, carried_capacity_without and service_load (total allocated demand);

  • distance_matrix and threshold used for the calculation.

objectnat.get_provision_buildings() and objectnat.get_provision_services() join these metrics to copies of the input GeoDataFrames; objectnat.get_provision_links() returns one straight line per non-zero flow, from the representative point of the building to the service, with building_index, service_index, demand (allocated units) and distance (cost).

objectnat.get_service_provision(*, buildings, distance_matrix, services, threshold, buildings_demand_column='demand', services_capacity_column='capacity', seed=0)[source]¶

Compute service provision between demand locations and service facilities.

The function implements a gravity-based allocation model: service capacity is distributed across nearby demand points with weights that decay with the square of distance (or generalized cost). Closer buildings receive proportionally higher shares of the available capacity. Only building-service pairs within 3 * threshold take part in the allocation, which runs in ObjectNat’s Rust extension over those pairs only.

Parameters:

  • buildings (GeoDataFrame) – GeoDataFrame of demand locations. Must include a numeric demand column.

  • distance_matrix (DataFrame) – OD cost matrix where rows are buildings.index and columns are services.index. Units must match threshold. Either a dense DataFrame (inf/NaN = unreachable) or, for large areas, a pandas sparse DataFrame (SparseDtype with a NaN or inf fill value) that stores only the reachable pairs.

  • services (GeoDataFrame) – GeoDataFrame of service facilities. Must include a numeric capacity column.

  • threshold (float) – Normative cost threshold. Units are the same as in distance_matrix.

  • buildings_demand_column (str) – Column name of building demand values. Default is "demand".

  • services_capacity_column (str) – Column name of service capacity values. Default is "capacity".

  • seed (int) – Seed of the random draws in the allocation. A fixed seed yields reproducible results (independent of the number of CPU cores); vary it to sample alternative allocations. Default is 0.

Returns:

Dataclass with a sparse building-service flow matrix, building metrics, service metrics, the aligned distance matrix, and the provision threshold.

Return type:

ProvisionResult

class objectnat.ProvisionResult(flow, demand_rows, capacity_rows, distance_matrix, threshold)[source]¶

Result of a service provision calculation.

Parameters:

  • flow (DataFrame)

  • demand_rows (DataFrame)

  • capacity_rows (DataFrame)

  • distance_matrix (DataFrame)

  • threshold (float)

flow¶

Sparse building-service allocation matrix. Rows match building indices, columns match service indices, and non-zero values are allocated demand units.

demand_rows¶

Per-building metrics indexed like flow.index. Includes original demand, remaining demand, supplied demand within/beyond the threshold, the cost to the nearest service, the flow-weighted mean cost and provision value.

capacity_rows¶

Per-service metrics indexed like flow.columns. Includes original capacity, remaining capacity, carried capacity within/beyond the threshold, and total service load.

distance_matrix¶

Aligned OD cost matrix used for the calculation (dense, or pandas sparse when a sparse matrix was passed). Its index and columns must match flow.

threshold¶

Normative distance or time threshold used to split flows into within-threshold and beyond-threshold metrics.

Recalculation and clipping¶

objectnat.recalculate_links() removes the flows whose cost exceeds new_max_dist and rebuilds the aggregate metrics without a new allocation. The removed demand is not redistributed to other services; run objectnat.get_service_provision() again for a full reallocation.

objectnat.clip_provision() keeps the buildings that intersect selection_zone, the links of these buildings and the services the links refer to. All four inputs must share one CRS.

Limitations¶

  • The allocation is stochastic: individual flows depend on seed; aggregate metrics vary little between seeds for large inputs but can vary for small ones.

  • Demand and capacity are integers (allocated in units); fractional values are truncated.

  • The model has no explicit choice behaviour (quality, preference, catchment rules); every reachable service within 3 * threshold can receive demand.

  • The result is only as accurate as the cost matrix; the example matrix contains travel times in minutes on an intermodal graph.
