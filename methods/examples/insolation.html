Insolation: sun hours on the ground and on facades¶

This notebook computes hours of direct sunlight among buildings of known height with get_insolation (ground receivers) and get_facade_insolation (facade points per floor):

  1. sun hours for a single day (equinox and summer solstice) as rasters (rasterize);

  2. a period (22 April – 22 August) with the longest continuous sunlit period on the worst day;

  3. the effect of terrain given as an array, and the raster on a web map (Raster.explore);

  4. facade insolation per floor and a check against a continuous-insolation norm.

The example buildings (Vasilyevsky Island, Saint Petersburg, 59.96° N) have a storeys_count column; heights are storeys × 3 m. The method is described on the insolation page.

The last section, Your own area, shows what to replace to get the same pictures for another territory.

# !pip install objectnat pyarrow matplotlib

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np
from mpl_toolkits.axes_grid1.anchored_artists import AnchoredSizeBar


def map_extent(ax, bounds, bar=100, outline=True):
    """Show `bounds` (x0, y0, x1, y1 in metres) without coordinate axes, with a scale bar and the area outline."""
    x0, y0, x1, y1 = bounds
    pad = 0.012 * max(x1 - x0, y1 - y0)
    ax.set_xlim(x0 - pad, x1 + pad)
    ax.set_ylim(y0 - pad, y1 + pad)
    ax.set_aspect("equal")
    ax.set_axis_off()
    if outline:
        ax.plot([x0, x1, x1, x0, x0], [y0, y0, y1, y1, y0], color="#3d3d3d", linewidth=1, zorder=8)
    scale = AnchoredSizeBar(ax.transData, bar, f"{bar:g} m", loc="lower right", pad=0.4, sep=4, size_vertical=bar / 40)
    scale.patch.set(facecolor="white", edgecolor="none", alpha=0.85)
    ax.add_artist(scale)

1. Study area¶

A 500 × 400 m residential area with courtyards. All buildings within 300 m of it are passed to the functions, because buildings outside the area also cast shadows into it.

import shapely

from objectnat import get_facade_insolation, get_insolation

area = shapely.box(344750, 6649850, 345250, 6650250)  # in the CRS of the buildings
scene = buildings[buildings.intersects(area.buffer(300))]
shown = scene.clip(area.buffer(1))
print(f"{len(scene)} buildings in the scene, {len(shown)} in the area")

171 buildings in the scene, 50 in the area

2. One day¶

date is a single day. Receivers are placed every grid_step metres outside footprints, and the sun position is sampled every time_step_min minutes. The result is the receiver points with sun_hours (0 for ground with no direct sun at all); attrs contain the simulated days, the day length and the grid. rasterize turns a column into a Raster on the grid of the calculation, whatever CRS the receivers are in, and plot draws it with matplotlib.

import time

import pandas as pd

days = {"2026-03-21": "21 March", "2026-06-21": "21 June"}
suns = {}
for day in days:
    start = time.perf_counter()
    suns[day] = get_insolation(scene, day, area=area, grid_step=2, time_step_min=10, **HEIGHTS)
    print(f"{day}: {time.perf_counter() - start:.1f} s, day length {suns[day].attrs['day_length_hours']:.1f} h")

pd.DataFrame({title: suns[day]["sun_hours"].describe() for day, title in days.items()}).round(2)

3. A period and the worst day¶

date=(start, end) simulates every day_step-th day of the period and its last day. The receiver points have sun_hours (mean over the simulated days), min_sun_hours (worst day) and max_continuous_hours (longest continuous sunlit period on the worst day), for which insolation norms usually set a minimum.

receivers = get_insolation(
    scene, ("2026-04-22", "2026-08-22"), area=area, grid_step=2, day_step=7, **HEIGHTS
)
print(len(receivers.attrs["days"]), "simulated days:", receivers.attrs["days"][0], "...", receivers.attrs["days"][-1])

4. Terrain¶

Terrain can be a GeoDataFrame of polygons with heights (rasterized with terrain_cell cells), a GeoTIFF path or a (heights, transform, crs) tuple; a Raster is such a tuple too. Here a synthetic ridge 50 m high runs west–east with its crest 50 m south of the area, given as an array with 5 m cells. Buildings stand on the terrain and receivers are placed on it, so a hill under the area itself changes little: what matters is terrain that rises above the line to the sun, here the ridge between the area and the low equinox sun.

from matplotlib import colors as mcolors

cell = 5.0
x0, y0, x1, y1 = area.buffer(400).bounds
cols, rows = int((x1 - x0) / cell), int((y1 - y0) / cell)
ys = y1 - (np.arange(rows) + 0.5) * cell  # row 0 is the northern edge
crest = area.bounds[1] - 50
ridge = 50.0 * np.exp(-((ys - crest) ** 2) / (2 * 30.0**2))
terrain = (np.repeat(ridge[:, None], cols, axis=1), (cell, 0.0, x0, 0.0, -cell, y1), buildings.crs)

flat = get_insolation(scene, "2026-03-21", area=area, grid_step=2, **HEIGHTS)
hilly = get_insolation(scene, "2026-03-21", area=area, grid_step=2, terrain=terrain, **HEIGHTS)
flat_r, hilly_r = rasterize(flat, "sun_hours"), rasterize(hilly, "sun_hours")
change = hilly_r.values - flat_r.values

fig, axs = plt.subplots(1, 3, figsize=(19, 5.2), constrained_layout=True)
for ax, raster, title in [(axs[0], flat_r, "21 March, flat ground"), (axs[1], hilly_r, "21 March, with the ridge")]:
    gpd.GeoSeries([area]).plot(ax=ax, color="#bdbdbd", zorder=0)
    im = raster.plot(ax=ax, cmap="inferno", vmin=0, vmax=12)
    shown.plot(ax=ax, color="#bdbdbd", edgecolor="#8c8c8c", linewidth=0.3)
    map_extent(ax, area.bounds)
    ax.set_title(title)
fig.colorbar(im, ax=axs[:2], shrink=0.8, label="direct sun, h/day")
gpd.GeoSeries([area]).plot(ax=axs[2], color="#bdbdbd", zorder=0)
im = type(flat_r)(change, flat_r.transform, flat_r.crs).plot(ax=axs[2], cmap="RdBu", norm=mcolors.TwoSlopeNorm(0, -6, 6))
shown.plot(ax=axs[2], color="#bdbdbd", edgecolor="#8c8c8c", linewidth=0.3)
map_extent(axs[2], area.bounds)
axs[2].set_title("ridge − flat")
fig.colorbar(im, ax=axs[2], shrink=0.8, label="change, h/day")

Rasters on a web map and in GIS¶

Raster.explore shows the raster on a folium map with a colour bar, like GeoDataFrame.explore(); pass a map as the first argument to add the layer to it. Web maps draw images in Web Mercator, so the grid is resampled to a Mercator grid first and lines up with the base map; cells without a value are transparent. Raster.to_folium returns the bare image overlay, and Raster.to_geotiff(path) writes a GeoTIFF for QGIS or ArcGIS (requires rasterio).

hilly_r.explore(cmap="inferno", vmin=0, vmax=12, opacity=0.8, name="direct sun on 21 March, h")

5. Facades per floor¶

get_facade_insolation places points every spacing metres along the walls of the buildings intersecting area, on every floor at window_height above the floor level. The sun counts while it is in front of the facade and the ray towards it is clear. norm_hours adds meets_norm, the check of the longest continuous sunlit period.

The example uses 22 April and 2.5 h of continuous sun as the norm value; use the calculation day and the value of the norm that applies to the project (for Russia, SanPiN 1.2.3685-21).

target = shapely.box(344900, 6649950, 345150, 6650200)
facades = get_facade_insolation(scene, "2026-04-22", area=target, spacing=3, window_height=1.2, norm_hours=2.5, **HEIGHTS)
print(len(facades), "facade points on", facades["building"].nunique(), "buildings")

6. Your own area¶

Building footprints with heights or storeys and an area. The buildings around the area cast shadows into it, so pass them too. Raster.plot draws the raster, Raster.explore shows it on a web map with a colour bar and Raster.to_geotiff writes a GeoTIFF for GIS (requires rasterio).

my_buildings = buildings                                  # your footprints; HEIGHTS names the height columns
my_area = shapely.box(344750, 6649850, 345250, 6650250)   # your area, in the CRS of the buildings
my_scene = my_buildings[my_buildings.intersects(my_area.buffer(300))]

sun = rasterize(get_insolation(my_scene, "2026-06-21", area=my_area, grid_step=2, **HEIGHTS),
                "sun_hours")
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(7, 5.6))
gpd.GeoSeries([my_area]).plot(ax=ax, color="#bdbdbd", zorder=0)  # cells without a receiver lie under buildings
im = sun.plot(ax=ax, cmap="inferno", vmin=0, vmax=19)
my_scene.clip(my_area).plot(ax=ax, color="#bdbdbd", linewidth=0)
map_extent(ax, my_area.bounds)
fig.colorbar(im, ax=ax, shrink=0.8, label="direct sun on 21 June, h")
plt.show()

Limitations¶

Roofs are flat; balconies, loggias, window reveals and trees are not modelled, and only direct sunlight is counted. Values are multiples of time_step_min. See the insolation page for details.
