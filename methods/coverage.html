Coverage Zones¶

Overview¶

A coverage zone is the area served by one service (a destination) within a travel-time or distance budget, where each place is assigned to the service that is closest along the network. Coverage differs from an isochrone in two respects: the network search runs towards the services (on the reversed graph), and zones of different services do not overlap.

  • objectnat.get_graph_coverage() builds one zone per service on an iduedu.UrbanGraph;

  • objectnat.get_stepped_graph_coverage() splits the covered area into bands of width step;

  • objectnat.get_radius_coverage() is a graph-free variant: a Voronoi partition of the services limited by a fixed radius.

Method¶

  1. Every service is snapped to its nearest graph node, or node ids are taken from graph_node_column or destination_nodes.

  2. A multi-source Dijkstra search runs on the reversed graph from all services at once, so every node is labelled with the service it reaches at the lowest cost (time_min in minutes or length_meter in metres) within weight_value_cutoff.

  3. Voronoi cells are built around the graph nodes, and the cells of the nodes labelled with one service are dissolved into its zone.

  4. The zones are clipped to zone (for example, a city boundary) or, without it, to the concave hull of the reachable nodes. geometry_type="radius" additionally clips them to buffers around the reachable nodes by the residual distance, at most off_network_distance (150 m by default, as for isochrones), and geometry_type="ways" to the buffered streets. For time_min the residual time is converted to a distance with the median edge speed of the graph.

On walk and intermodal graphs only pedestrian (type == "walk") edges shape "ways", and only their end nodes shape any geometry, as for isochrones.

Stepped coverage labels every node with the cost to its nearest service and builds one band per cost interval of width step: Voronoi cells (geometry_type=None), cells clipped to residual-radius buffers or streets ("radius", "ways") or independent buffers ("separate").

objectnat.get_radius_coverage() reprojects the services to a local UTM zone, builds their Voronoi polygons, clips them to the union of radius-metre buffers around the services and smooths every polygon by an inward buffer followed by a slightly smaller outward buffer, both proportional to the cube root of the polygon area. The smoothing rounds the corners, so zones can be slightly smaller than the clipped Voronoi cells.

Outputs¶

objectnat.get_graph_coverage() and objectnat.get_radius_coverage() return one polygon per service, indexed like the input services (join service attributes back by index). objectnat.get_stepped_graph_coverage() returns polygons with the column dist (upper bound of the interval, in minutes or metres). All results are in the CRS of the input services.

objectnat.get_graph_coverage(urban_graph, *, gdf_destinations=None, destination_nodes=None, graph_node_column='graph_node_id', weight_type='time_min', geometry_type=None, weight_value_cutoff=None, zone=None, buffer_factor=0.7, road_buffer_size=5.0, off_network_distance=150.0)[source]¶

Calculate coverage zones of destinations (services) on an urban graph using Dijkstra reachability and Voronoi partitioning.

Coverage answers “which area is served by each destination”. The function:

  1. Snaps each destination to its nearest graph node (or uses the provided destination_nodes).

  2. Runs a multi-source Dijkstra search on the reversed graph, so every reachable node is labelled with its nearest destination within weight_value_cutoff.

  3. Builds Voronoi polygons around the graph nodes.

  4. Dissolves the reachable Voronoi cells per destination into one zone each.

  5. Clips the result to zone, to a residual-radius / road geometry (geometry_type), or to the concave hull of the reachable nodes.

Parameters:

  • urban_graph (UrbanGraph) – City graph with node (nodes_gdf) and edge (edges_gdf) tables.

  • gdf_destinations (geopandas.GeoDataFrame, optional) – Destinations the coverage is measured to. If the table already contains graph_node_column those node ids are used directly; otherwise each geometry is snapped to its nearest graph node. Pass either this or destination_nodes.

  • destination_nodes (Iterable, optional) – Node ids of the destinations, used instead of gdf_destinations.

  • graph_node_column (str) – Name of the column with graph node ids in gdf_destinations.

  • weight_type (Literal['time_min', 'length_meter']) –

    Type of edge weight used for path calculation:

    • "time_min": edge travel time in minutes

    • "length_meter": edge length in metres

  • geometry_type (Optional[Literal['radius', 'ways']]) –

    Optional refinement of the coverage shape:

    • None: raw Voronoi cells clipped to zone or the concave hull

    • "radius": additionally clip to buffers around the reachable nodes (remaining budget as a distance, at most off_network_distance)

    • "ways": additionally clip to buffered road geometry inside the residual radii (walk edges only on intermodal/walk graphs)

    Requires weight_value_cutoff when set.

  • weight_value_cutoff (float, optional) – Maximum path cost, e.g. max travel time or distance. Units depend on weight_type.

  • zone (gpd.GeoDataFrame | gpd.GeoSeries, optional) – Boundary polygon to clip the resulting zones. If None and no geometry_type is given, the concave hull of the reachable nodes is used.

  • buffer_factor (float) – Not used by coverage zones; accepted for symmetry with get_stepped_graph_coverage() (default 0.7).

  • road_buffer_size (float) – Buffer applied to graph edges for geometry_type="ways", in metres (default 5.0).

  • off_network_distance (float | None, optional) – For geometry_type="radius": how far from the network, in metres, a place may lie and still count as reached. Every reachable node is buffered by its remaining budget converted to a distance, but by no more than this, so the area follows the network instead of growing into a straight-line circle around the start. None buffers by the whole remaining budget. Defaults to 150.

Returns:

One coverage polygon per destination, returned in the CRS of gdf_destinations (or the graph CRS when node ids are passed). The index matches gdf_destinations / destination_nodes.

Return type:

gpd.GeoDataFrame

Notes

  • For a directed graph the search runs on the reversed edges, so a zone describes the area from which its destination can be reached.

  • On walk and intermodal graphs the zones are built from nodes reachable on foot, so route nodes sharing a stop cannot give one place to several destinations.

  • An empty GeoDataFrame is returned when nothing is reachable.

objectnat.get_stepped_graph_coverage(urban_graph, *, gdf_destinations=None, destination_nodes=None, graph_node_column='graph_node_id', weight_type='time_min', geometry_type='radius', weight_value_cutoff=None, zone=None, step=None, buffer_factor=0.7, road_buffer_size=5.0, off_network_distance=150.0)[source]¶

Calculate stepped coverage zones of destinations (services), combining graph reachability with banded (stepped) isochrone geometry.

Like get_graph_coverage(), but instead of one zone per destination the reachable area is split into concentric bands of width step. The function:

  1. Snaps each destination to its nearest graph node (or uses the provided destination_nodes).

  2. Runs a multi-source Dijkstra search on the reversed graph, labelling every reachable node with the cost to its nearest destination.

  3. Buckets the nodes into steps and builds banded geometry with the selected geometry_type.

  4. Optionally clips the bands to zone.

Parameters:

  • urban_graph (UrbanGraph) – City graph with node and edge tables.

  • gdf_destinations (geopandas.GeoDataFrame, optional) – Destinations the coverage is measured to. If the table contains graph_node_column those ids are used; otherwise geometries are snapped to nearest nodes. Pass either this or destination_nodes.

  • destination_nodes (Iterable, optional) – Node ids of the destinations, used instead of gdf_destinations.

  • graph_node_column (str) – Name of the graph node id column in gdf_destinations.

  • weight_type (Literal['time_min', 'length_meter']) –

    Type of edge weight used for path calculation:

    • "time_min": edge travel time in minutes

    • "length_meter": edge length in metres

  • geometry_type (Optional[Literal['radius', 'ways', 'separate']]) –

    Method used to build each step’s geometry:

    • None: Voronoi cells around graph nodes

    • "radius": Voronoi cells clipped to the radius buffers (see off_network_distance)

    • "ways": Voronoi cells clipped to buffered road geometry (walk edges only on intermodal/walk graphs)

    • "separate": independent circular buffers per step

  • weight_value_cutoff (float, optional) – Maximum path cost limiting the coverage extent. If None, the farthest reachable node defines the extent.

  • zone (gpd.GeoDataFrame | gpd.GeoSeries, optional) – Boundary polygon to clip the resulting stepped zones.

  • step (float, optional) – Width of each step, in units of weight_type. Defaults to 100 metres for length_meter and 1 minute for time_min.

  • buffer_factor (float) – Buffer multiplier used by geometry_type="separate" (default 0.7).

  • road_buffer_size (float) – Edge buffer for geometry_type="ways", in metres (default 5.0).

  • off_network_distance (float | None, optional) – For geometry_type="radius": how far from the network, in metres, a place may lie and still count as reached. Every reachable node is buffered by its remaining budget converted to a distance, but by no more than this, so the area follows the network instead of growing into a straight-line circle around the start. None buffers by the whole remaining budget. Defaults to 150.

Returns:

Stepped coverage polygons with a dist column (the upper bound of each step, in units of weight_type) and geometry, returned in the CRS of gdf_destinations (or the graph CRS).

Return type:

gpd.GeoDataFrame

Notes

  • For a directed graph the search runs on the reversed edges; for an undirected graph on the original symmetric adjacency.

  • On walk and intermodal graphs the bands are built from nodes reachable on foot, so transit route nodes at a stop, whose cost includes the wait, do not move it to a later band.

  • An empty GeoDataFrame is returned when nothing is reachable.

objectnat.get_radius_coverage(gdf_from, radius, resolution=32)[source]¶

Calculate radius-based coverage zones using Voronoi polygons.

This is a graph-free coverage: zones are built purely from the service points, a fixed radius and a Voronoi partition, with no network involved.

Parameters:

  • gdf_from (geopandas.GeoDataFrame) – Service points for which coverage zones are calculated.

  • radius (float) – Maximum coverage radius in metres.

  • resolution (int) – Number of segments per quarter circle of the buffers (default 32).

Returns:

Smoothed coverage zones, one polygon per point, in the CRS of gdf_from.

Return type:

gpd.GeoDataFrame

Notes

  • The calculation runs in a local UTM zone, so radius is in metres.

  • The zones are smoothed by a slight inward and outward buffer, which rounds their corners.

Figures¶

Limitations¶

  • Each place is assigned to one service, the one with the lowest network cost; capacity is not considered. Use Service Provision to allocate demand under capacity limits.

  • Zone outlines between network nodes come from Voronoi cells and residual-distance buffers, not from the street layout; their accuracy depends on the density of graph nodes.

  • objectnat.get_radius_coverage() ignores the network and barriers such as rivers or railways.
